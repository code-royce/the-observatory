## Project Summary

The Light Pollution Analytics and Celestial Visibility Planner is a web application designed to help amateur astronomers, astrophotographers, and general stargazers make the most of their observation time by pairing environmental data with celestial tracking. The primary problem this application solves is the unpredictable nature of stargazing due to urban light pollution. Finding a "dark sky" location alone, however, doesn’t address most users’ structural knowledge gap regarding which specific stars, constellations, or deep-sky objects will actually be visible to the naked eye or through a lens from a given location or on a specific night.

As a solution, our application integrates two large-scale public datasets. The first dataset consists of geographic light pollution data, which maps limiting magnitudes to specific coordinates and ZIP codes. The second dataset is a comprehensive star catalog, containing tens of thousands of rows tracking celestial coordinates, brightness magnitudes, and object classifications. By joining these datasets, the application allows users to discover the best nearby observation points and instantly see an accurate, filtered list of visible celestial targets.
